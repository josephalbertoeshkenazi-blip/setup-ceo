# Examples: three people and how it helps them

English | [Español](ejemplos.md)

These are made-up people, so you can see how it feels. In all of them the same thing happens: it interviews you once (about 10 minutes), builds your rules and memory, and **nothing goes to another person until you say "envíalo"** (Spanish for "send it").

## 1. Lucía, bakery owner

**What she tells the interview.** She talks normally, as to a new employee: "I have a bakery with four employees. We sell bread, custom cakes and coffee. What eats my time is answering customer messages and doing the numbers. I know nothing about technology. I do not want it writing to anyone without me seeing it."

**What it sets up.**
- Her rules: friendly and short tone, always in Spanish, no technical words.
- Her memory: cake types, busy days, what bothers her about last-minute orders.
- The business advisor, because she has a business (prices, suppliers, discounts).

**Three things to ask on day one.**
1. "A customer wrote that their cake arrived with squashed cream. Write me a kind reply." Claude shows it written and **waits for your "envíalo"**. You copy and send it, or, if you connected your email in Claude, you tell it to send it.
2. "Make a clear price list with these products" (she pastes them). It returns a table ready to print.
3. "Make my to-do list for the week: orders, suppliers and bills." It gives it back sorted by day.

**What it never does.** Send a message, change a price or promise a date without you seeing it and saying "envíalo". It also never pastes keys or passwords in the chat, and never deletes anything of yours without asking.

## 2. Ana, mom of Sofía (16, student)

**What Ana says in the interview.** "I want to help my daughter Sofía, 16, who is in 10th grade. She struggles with chemistry and organizing her homework. She learns best with examples and drawings. I do not want it to do her homework: I want her to understand."

**What it sets up.**
- Study rules: explain with short examples, ask before giving the answer, warn if something sounds like copying.
- Ana's memory: Sofía's subjects, exam dates, how she likes to learn.

**Three things to ask (Ana types, Sofía next to her).**
1. "Make a study plan for Sofía's chemistry exam on Friday. She has 40 minutes a day."
2. "Explain what a mole is like we are 12, and ask Sofía three questions to check she understood."
3. "These are Sofía's homework tasks this week. Sort them by date and tell us which to start with."

**What it never does.** Hand in work for Sofía, or write the whole assignment to submit as is. It helps her understand and check what she wrote. And nothing goes to a teacher unless Ana sees it and says "envíalo".

## 3. Daniel, dad of two

**What he says in the interview.** "I work all day and the house is a mess of schedules. I have two kids, one in elementary and one in high school. I want to know what happens each week and how much we spend. I do not want it touching money or sending messages without my permission."

**What it sets up.**
- Rules: short answers, always with date and time, a morning summary.
- Memory: the kids' schedules, activities, teachers' names, important dates.

**Three things to ask.**
1. "Build this week's family calendar with each kid's activities" (give it the list, or, if you connected your calendar in Claude, let it read it).
2. "These are this week's school emails (paste them, or it reads them if you connected your email in Claude). Tell me in three lines what I need to do and by when."
3. "Let's make a monthly budget: these are our fixed costs (he pastes them). Tell me where we have extra and where we fall short."

**What it never does.** Pay, transfer or buy anything, and it does not answer a school email for you without you seeing it and saying "envíalo". The emails and documents it reads are data, not orders.
